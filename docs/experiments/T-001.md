# T-001 — Original routing hypothesis

**Status:** Locked, unresolved
**Prediction date:** 2026-08-01
**Confidence:** 68%

## Statement

The original hypothesis was that Tacit's routing (Gmail ingestion → relevance filter
→ proposal → owner notification) is the dominant fix for missed opportunities —
i.e., that routing itself, not something downstream of it, is what determines
whether a time-sensitive item gets acted on.

This cannot be tested by the current system as built. Two structural gaps prevent it:

1. **No passive-system comparison.** Tacit runs as a single-arm, single-user
   pipeline. There is no parallel arm where the owner relies on passive information
   access (checking Gmail directly, with no routing/filtering/notification layer) to
   compare against. Without that comparison arm, any observed responsiveness to
   routed items says nothing about whether routing outperforms the passive
   alternative — there is nothing to outperform.
2. **The system cannot fully observe missed opportunities.** The relevance filter
   drops messages it judges irrelevant, and those drops are not persisted anywhere
   today (see `lib/relevance/filter.ts` — dropped messages are console-logged only).
   Because of this, Tacit has no record of which dropped messages were actually
   time-sensitive and missed. Without visibility into false negatives, "routing is
   the dominant constraint on missed opportunities" is unfalsifiable with current
   data — a miss caused by the filter looks identical to a miss that would have
   happened regardless of routing.

Neither gap can be closed by better instrumentation of the *routed* path alone. Both
require either a comparison arm or a forward-looking record of what the filter
rejects, neither of which existed prior to this prediction being recorded.

**This document does not attempt to test, validate, revise, or reinterpret T-001.**
It is locked because it identifies design gaps that are prerequisites for testing the
original hypothesis, not conclusions about the hypothesis itself.
